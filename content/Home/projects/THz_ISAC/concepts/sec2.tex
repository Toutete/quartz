\section{Proposed SI-Referenced Full-Duplex ISAC System}
\label{sec:system_model}

We consider a photonic terahertz (THz) full-duplex integrated sensing
and communication (ISAC) system in which a single aperture
simultaneously delivers data over a one-way line-of-sight communication
link and senses a target in a monostatic configuration. The transmit and
echo paths are separated by circular-polarization duplexing through an
orthomode transducer (OMT); the finite OMT isolation produces a residual
self-interference (SI) leakage. Rather than suppressing this leakage, as
in conventional full-duplex sensing, the proposed receiver reuses it as a
self-homodyne reference for range estimation, which is the central idea
of this work and is detailed in Sec.~\ref{sec:cfr}. The architecture is
shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}. The communication and sensing links
share the transmitted waveform but use physically distinct receivers,
and are analyzed separately in
Secs.~\ref{sec:comm_branch}--\ref{sec:sens_branch} after a common
transmit and detection model.

\subsection{Transmit Signal Model}
\label{sec:tx_signal}

Two free-running lasers are combined and photomixed in a
uni-travelling-carrier photodiode (UTC-PD), whose output is a THz-band
voltage proportional to the instantaneous optical intensity beat. One
optical branch is intensity-modulated by a Mach--Zehnder modulator
(MZM). The transmitted THz voltage is
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{THz}}(t)=A_c\big[1+m\,s(t)\big]\,
e^{j\left(2\pi f_c t+\Delta\theta(t)\right)},
\label{eq:vthz}
\end{equation}
where $A_c$ is the carrier amplitude, $m$ the modulation index, $s(t)$
the baseband symbol stream. Here $f_c$ is the beat frequency. Because
the two lasers are free-running, both a fast common phase noise
$\Delta\theta(t)$ and a slow drift of $f_c$ are present; neither is
locked to any reference. This is the key impairment the receiver must
tolerate.

Throughout this section, $A_c$ is a normalized carrier coefficient and
does not contain a second factor of the transmitted power $P_t$. For
compactness, the factor $\sqrt{P_t}$ is absorbed into the received-field
coefficients defined below. This convention is equivalent to the
implementation: \texttt{isac\_gui.py} first normalizes the generated
photomixed waveform to $P_t$ and subsequently applies only dimensionless
SI and propagation coefficients. Hence, $P_t$ is applied exactly once.

\subsubsection{Waveform}
The free-running beat drift in~\eqref{eq:vthz} precludes time-domain
matched filtering, whose correlation peak fades as the carrier drifts;
range must instead be estimated from the channel frequency response
(CFR), which is drift-immune as shown in Sec.~\ref{sec:cfr}. The
waveform is therefore drawn from the orthogonal frequency-division
multiplexing (OFDM) family, which pairs naturally with per-subcarrier
CFR estimation and frequency-domain equalization. After synchronization,
one complex equalization coefficient is applied to each active frequency
bin, i.e., $\widehat X[k]=Y[k]/\widehat H[k]$, before IDFT despreading.
This conventional one-tap FDE is distinct from the optional symbol-domain
scalar gain/phase correction used only before EVM evaluation. Within this family, the
transmit power at THz frequencies is strongly constrained by the
peak-to-average power ratio (PAPR): the saturated output power of power
amplifiers falls rapidly with carrier frequency, so a lower PAPR is
essential to raise the average transmit power \cite{Han2023}. We
therefore adopt discrete Fourier transform-spread OFDM (DFT-s-OFDM)
over cyclic-prefix OFDM (CP-OFDM). As a single-carrier waveform,
DFT-s-OFDM exhibits a PAPR about $2$~dB lower than multi-carrier
CP-OFDM \cite{Han2023}, while retaining the same CFR compatibility;
the $2$~dB reduction translates directly into a $2$~dB higher average
transmit power under the same peak-power limit, a significant gain on a
power-scarce THz link.

To keep the system model independent of implementation-specific optical
filtering reference planes, the carrier-to-signal power ratio (CSPR) is
defined directly for the effective transmitted THz waveform as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CSPR}=\frac{P_c}{P_s}=\frac{1}{m^2}.
\label{eq:effective_cspr}
\end{equation}
The measured operating value $\mathrm{CSPR}=13$~dB therefore gives
$m=10^{-13/20}=0.2239$. This same effective modulation index is used
consistently in the transmitted-signal, ZBD, SSBI, and SINR equations.
The experimental optical filtering arrangement is described separately
with the hardware setup and is absorbed here into the calibrated CSPR
and transmitted power $P_t$.

\subsubsection{Power Allocation}
The waveform carries a deterministic sensing pilot and the communication
payload,
\begin{equation}
s(t)=\sqrt{\rho}\,p(t)+\sqrt{1-\rho}\,d(t),\qquad
\mathbb E\big[|s(t)|^2\big]=1,
\label{eq:st}
\end{equation}
where $p(t)$ is a unit-power constant-modulus Zadoff--Chu pilot, $d(t)$
the unit-power data symbols, and $\rho\in[0,1]$ the sensing-communication
power-split ratio. Both functions are thus carried by a single shared
waveform, preserving full temporal overlap of communication and sensing.
The power split $\rho$ is nearly independent of the peak-to-average power
ratio and hence does not affect the back-off; it instead trades the two
links' maximum ranges, as quantified in Sec.~\ref{sec:isac_range}.

\subsection{Received Signal Model}
\label{sec:rx_signal}

At the sensing port of the OMT, the transmit leakage $V_{\mathrm{SI}}$,
of delay approximately zero, superimposes on the target echo
$V_{\mathrm{ec}}$, of round-trip delay $\tau=2R/c$,
\begin{align}
V_{\mathrm{SI}}(t)&=\alpha\,V_{\mathrm{THz}}(t),\qquad
V_{\mathrm{ec}}(t)=\beta\,V_{\mathrm{THz}}(t-\tau),\label{eq:vsi_vec}\\
V_{\mathrm{rx}}(t)&=V_{\mathrm{SI}}(t)+V_{\mathrm{ec}}(t),\label{eq:vrx}
\end{align}
where $\alpha=\sqrt{P_t}\alpha_0$, with
$\alpha_0=10^{-\mathrm{ISO}/20}$ denoting the dimensionless OMT
leakage coefficient, and $\beta=\sqrt{K}/R^2$ is the echo amplitude,
with
$K=P_tG_tG_r\lambda^2\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}/(4\pi)^3$. In practice
$\alpha\gg\beta$, so $V_{\mathrm{rx}}$ is SI-dominated. A THz low-noise
amplifier (LNA) precedes the detector; the OMT isolation is designed so
that the SI power stays below the LNA input $1$-dB compression point,
$P_{\mathrm{SI}}<\mathrm{IP}_{1\mathrm{dB}}$, keeping the front end
linear.

For the communication link there is no self-interference, and the
received field at the remote receiver is
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{rx},c}(t)=\beta_c\,V_{\mathrm{THz}}(t-\tau_c),
\label{eq:rc}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_c^2=\eta_L P_tG_c/R^2$ is the one-way received-field coefficient
under the normalized-$A_c$ convention, $\eta_L=1-|\Gamma|^2$ is the
fraction of incident power accepted by the target communication antenna,
and $\tau_c$ the
propagation delay. The remote communication receiver also serves as the
reflecting target for the sensing link.
For this antenna target, the link-budget RCS is modeled as
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}\simeq\sigma_{\mathrm{str}}+
\frac{\lambda^2G_{\mathrm{tar}}^2}{4\pi}|\Gamma|^2\eta_{\mathrm{pol}},
\label{eq:gamma_rcs}
\end{equation}
where the sum is an incoherent-power approximation used when the relative
phase of structural- and antenna-mode scattering is unavailable.  The same
$\Gamma$ is used in $\eta_L$ and in the antenna-mode term, preventing the
communication and sensing branches from independently assuming perfect
absorption and maximum reradiation.  For a measured or generic target, a
direct $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}$ can replace~\eqref{eq:gamma_rcs}; it is then a
scenario parameter and is not inverted to infer load mismatch.

\subsection{Square-Law Detection}
\label{sec:squarelaw}

Both links use a square-law envelope detector, a zero-bias Schottky
diode (ZBD), with output
$V_{\mathrm{SLD}}(t)=\mathcal R\,|V_{\mathrm{in}}(t)|^2$. As a
single-port passive rectifier the ZBD needs no local oscillator, and the
carrier phase together with the common laser phase noise cancels in the
squaring. For a generic input
$\gamma A_c[1+m\,s(t-\tau)]e^{j(2\pi f_c(t-\tau)+\Delta\theta)}$ with
$\gamma\in\{\alpha,\beta,\beta_c\}$, the output separates by power of
$m$,
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{SLD}}(t)=\mathcal R\gamma^2A_c^2\Big[1
+2m\,\mathrm{Re}\{s(t-\tau)\}+m^2|s(t-\tau)|^2\Big],
\label{eq:vout_decomp}
\end{equation}
comprising a direct-current term, a linear intermediate-frequency term
of order $m$, and a signal-signal beat interference term of order $m^2$,
denoted SSBI. A direct-current block removes the flat term, which for the
sensing link also absorbs the time-invariant carrier-carrier product,
and an intermediate-frequency bandpass retains the linear term. The SSBI
arises from the detector squaring the sideband against itself; it spreads
over twice the signal bandwidth, and the portion re-entering the passband
cannot be filtered out and acts as co-channel interference.

Applying~\eqref{eq:vout_decomp} to the SI-plus-echo input
$V_{\mathrm{rx}}$ and keeping the SI-echo cross term, which carries the
range, gives the sensing intermediate-frequency signal
\begin{align}
V_{\mathrm{SLD}}(t)\big|_{\mathrm{IF}}=\mathcal R A_c^2 m\big[
&\alpha\,s(t)\notag\\
&+\beta\,e^{j2\pi f_c\tau}s(t-\tau)\big]+n(t),
\label{eq:yt}
\end{align}
the SI-borne copy at zero delay plus the echo-borne copy at delay $\tau$.

\subsection{SI-Referenced Range Estimation}
\label{sec:cfr}

This subsection describes the core of the proposed system: the SI
leakage, normally an impairment, serves as the self-homodyne reference
that makes free-running square-law ranging possible.

Dividing the intermediate-frequency spectrum of~\eqref{eq:yt} by the
known transmit spectrum $S(f)$ yields the CFR
\begin{align}
H(f)&=\alpha'+\beta'\,e^{-j2\pi(f+f_c)\tau},\notag\\
\alpha'&=\mathcal R A_c^2 m\alpha,\qquad
\beta'=\mathcal R A_c^2 m\beta,
\label{eq:cfr}
\end{align}
a frequency-flat SI term plus a delay-carrying echo term. Estimating the
flat component as the amplitude-weighted mean
$H_{\mathrm{SI}}=\sum_f w(f)H(f)/\sum_f w(f)\approx\alpha'$ and
normalizing removes the common carrier amplitude, modulation index, and
per-capture laser phase in a single step,
\begin{equation}
\tilde H(f)=\frac{H(f)}{H_{\mathrm{SI}}}-1
=\frac{\beta}{\alpha}\,e^{-j2\pi(f+f_c)\tau}.
\label{eq:sinorm}
\end{equation}
The range is obtained from the inverse discrete Fourier transform of the
residual,
\begin{equation}
P(\tau')=\Big|\sum_f w(f)\,\tilde H'(f)\,e^{j2\pi f\tau'}\Big|,\qquad
\hat R=\frac{c}{2}\hat\tau,
\label{eq:idft}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde H'$ is the direct-current-removed residual and $\hat\tau$
the location of the peak of $P(\tau')$. Because the SI is estimated and
removed before profiling, the range profile is free of an SI peak or its
sidelobes. Moreover, the free-running beat drift $f_c$ enters
\eqref{eq:cfr} only as a common phase that cancels in the ratio
\eqref{eq:sinorm}, so the estimate is immune to carrier drift; a single
capture suffices, preserving moving targets.

This carrier-drift immunity is why CFR, and not time-domain matched
filtering, is essential here. The single square-law detector yields a
real, single-quadrature waveform, for which time-domain matched
filtering against a fixed template measures only the projection of the
per-capture phase $2\pi f_c\tau$ onto the template, so the correlation
peak fades from capture to capture as $f_c$ drifts. The frequency-domain
response of the same real signal is complex at every bin, and the drift
enters only as the common phase removed in \eqref{eq:sinorm}, so the
range estimate is recovered without any local oscillator or optical
frequency reference.

\subsection{Communication Branch}
\label{sec:comm_branch}

Applying~\eqref{eq:vout_decomp} to the one-way field~\eqref{eq:rc}, the
desired data power and the in-band SSBI power are
\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{sig}}=2\mathcal R^2\beta_c^4A_c^4m^2(1-\rho),\qquad
P_{\mathrm{SSBI}}=\mathcal R^2\beta_c^4A_c^4m^4\xi,
\label{eq:psig_pssbi}
\end{equation}
where $\xi=\mathbb E\big[\big||s(t)|^2\ast h_{\mathrm{BPF}}(t)\big|^2\big]$
is the fraction of the self-beat spectrum leaking into the passband, set
by the waveform and minimized by the DFT-s-OFDM choice of
Sec.~\ref{sec:tx_signal}. With $N_c$ the receiver noise power and
$P_{\mathrm{rx}}\triangleq\mathcal R^2\beta_c^4A_c^4=\mathcal
R^2A_c^4G_c^2/R^4$, the communication signal-to-interference-plus-noise
ratio, denoted SINR, is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SINR}_{\mathrm{comm}}(m,\rho,R)
=\frac{2P_{\mathrm{rx}}\,m^2(1-\rho)}{N_c+P_{\mathrm{rx}}\,m^4\xi}.
\label{eq:sinr_comm}
\end{equation}
The required $\mathrm{SINR}_{\mathrm{comm}}=\gamma_c$, equivalently a
target error-vector magnitude for a given pre-forward-error-correction
bit-error rate, defines the maximum communication range
$R_{\max}^{\mathrm{comm}}(m,\rho)$.

Lowering CSPR increases the desired term as $m^2$ but increases SSBI as
$m^4$, whereas an excessively high CSPR makes the link noise limited.
For the simplified SINR in~\eqref{eq:sinr_comm}, its stationary point
satisfies $P_{\mathrm{rx}}m_{\mathrm{opt}}^4\xi=N_c$, i.e., the in-band
SSBI and receiver-noise terms are equal. The maximizing CSPR is therefore
not universal: it depends on the noise
level, received power, waveform-dependent leakage factor $\xi$, and
constellation moments. We use the calibrated effective value of
$13$~dB as the fixed operating point for the reported experiment and
simulation, rather than claiming it as a system-independent optimum.

\subsection{Sensing Branch}
\label{sec:sens_branch}

In a coherent receiver driven by an independent local oscillator the SI
would enter the SINR denominator as interference and, with
$\alpha\gg\beta$, bury the echo. In the square-law ZBD the ranging term
is instead the SI--echo cross-beat of~\eqref{eq:yt}, so the SI acts as a
homodyne local oscillator in the numerator.  Let $P_{\mathrm{SI}}$ and
$P_{\mathrm{ec}}(R)$ denote the SI and echo RF powers at the common ZBD
reference plane. After pilot correlation with effective processing gain
$G_{p,\mathrm{eff}}=\eta_pT_pB$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SINR}_{\mathrm{sens}}^{\mathrm{w/\ SI}}(m,\rho,R)
=G_{p,\mathrm{eff}}\frac{2m^2\rho
\left[P_{\mathrm{SI}}P_{\mathrm{ec}}(R)+P_{\mathrm{ec}}^2(R)\right]}
{P_0N},
\label{eq:sinr_sens}
\end{equation}
where $P_0=1$~mW is the power-normalization reference used by the
numerical model and $N=kTBF$ is the equivalent input-referred noise
power.  Equivalently, the code evaluates every power numerically in mW;
the explicit $P_0$ prevents a dimensionally invalid power-squared over
power expression. The leakage-generated SSBI is
range-independent and of order $m^4$; at the reported operating point it
is below the noise floor and is omitted from this sensing bound. Without
the SI reference, the echo is detected only through its own self-beat,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SINR}_{\mathrm{sens}}^{\mathrm{w/o\ SI}}(m,\rho,R)
=G_{p,\mathrm{eff}}\frac{2m^2\rho P_{\mathrm{ec}}^2(R)}
{P_0N}.
\label{eq:sinr_sens_nosi}
\end{equation}
Consequently, the exact phase-averaged with-SI to without-SI ratio is
$1+P_{\mathrm{SI}}/P_{\mathrm{ec}}(R)$ in the noise-limited regime.
The commonly quoted $P_{\mathrm{SI}}/P_{\mathrm{ec}}(R)$ result drops the
self-beat from the with-SI numerator and is only the
$P_{\mathrm{SI}}\gg P_{\mathrm{ec}}$ approximation. Retaining both positive
power terms guarantees that adding the SI reference cannot reduce the
phase-averaged sensing bound.

The ideal coherent time--bandwidth gain $G_{p,\mathrm{ideal}}=BT_p$ is
retained only as an upper bound. For a fixed $N_p$-symbol pilot,
$T_p=N_p/R_s$ and hence $G_{p,\mathrm{ideal}}=N_pB/R_s$; when
$B\simeq R_s$, the gain is approximately $N_p$ and equals $30.10$~dB
for $N_p=1024$. In the saved C2 capture, 908 CFR bins survive the mask and
their nonuniform weights give the equivalent count
$N_{\mathrm{eff}}=(\sum_k w_k)^2/\sum_k w_k^2=526.5$, or $27.21$~dB.
We therefore use a conservative assumed value
$G_{p,\mathrm{eff}}=26.0$~dB for both the sensing-SINR and ISAC-range
results. It is $4.10$~dB below the ideal bound and $1.21$~dB below the
weighted-bin bound, corresponding to $\eta_p=10^{-4.10/10}=0.389$.
The residual margin represents synchronization, phase-coherence,
reference-mismatch, and implementation loss; weighting and discarded
bins are already represented in $N_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and are not counted
again. Because this is an assumed operating value rather than a directly
measured universal gain, we use $24$--$28$~dB as a sensitivity interval.
The communication and sensing bounds use the same input-referred
thermal-noise model $N=kTBF$, with a common equivalent
receiver noise figure $F$ obtained from the simulated LNA/IF-amplifier
Friis cascade and without ZBD-NEP or quantization noise.

Under a distance-independent post-detection noise floor, the exact
SI-assisted ZBD output SINR is the sum of an $R^{-4}$ SI--echo term and an
$R^{-8}$ echo-self-beat term. At long range, where
$P_{\mathrm{SI}}\gg P_{\mathrm{ec}}$, the former dominates and gives the
usual homodyne $R^{-4}$ range-extension law. Without the SI reference only
the $R^{-8}$ self-beat remains. The $R^{-8}$ figure is specific to
square-law self-detection in the output domain, not the conventional
$R^{-4}$ radar signal-to-noise ratio defined at the RF input; the
 measured sensing SINR reported here is defined in the ZBD output domain.
The gain grows with the SI strength $\alpha$, but only until the front
end reaches $\mathrm{IP}_{1\mathrm{dB}}$; if $m$ is increased beyond the
reported operating region, the omitted leakage-generated SSBI must also
be reinstated. The maximum sensing range
$R_{\max}^{\mathrm{sens}}(m,\rho)$ solves
$\mathrm{SINR}_{\mathrm{sens}}=\gamma_{th}$, with $\gamma_{th}$ set by a
target detection operating point, for example $P_d=0.9$ at
$P_{fa}=10^{-6}$ for a single-look, non-fluctuating target.

\subsection{ISAC Range}
\label{sec:isac_range}

For the deterministic range plot, define
$P_{\mathrm{ec}}(R)=P_tK/R^4$,
$P_{\mathrm{SI}}=P_t\alpha_0^2$, and
$P_{\mathrm{rx},c}(R)=P_tG_c/R^2$, where $G_c$ already contains the
one-way Friis coefficient, insertion losses, and the load-acceptance
factor $1-|\Gamma|^2$. Define
$C=2\rho m^2G_{p,\mathrm{eff}}/(P_0N)$,
$A_4=CP_{\mathrm{SI}}P_tK$, and $A_8=C(P_tK)^2$. The noise-limited
range bounds implemented in the GUI are then
\begin{align}
R_{\max}^{\mathrm{comm}}
&=\left[\frac{(1-\rho)m^2P_tG_c}{N\gamma_c}\right]^{1/2},\notag\\
R_{\max}^{\mathrm{sens,w/\ SI}}
&=\left[\frac{A_4+\sqrt{A_4^2+4\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}A_8}}
{2\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}\right]^{1/4},\notag\\
R_{\max}^{\mathrm{sens,w/o\ SI}}
&=\left[\frac{A_8}{\gamma_{\mathrm{th}}}\right]^{1/8}.
\label{eq:range_bounds}
\end{align}
Thus effective RCS appears only through $K\propto\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}$:
both sensing ranges scale as $\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{1/4}$. The exact
with-SI sensitivity to a common SINR gain transitions between an
eighth-root self-beat limit and a fourth-root cross-beat limit. The
communication curve remains
constant on an effective-RCS sweep because $|\Gamma|$ is held fixed and
is not inferred back from the scenario-level RCS.

The ISAC range is
\begin{equation}
R_{\max}(m,\rho)=\min\big(R_{\max}^{\mathrm{comm}}(m,\rho),\,
R_{\max}^{\mathrm{sens}}(m,\rho)\big),
\label{eq:rmax}
\end{equation}
with $R_{\max}^{\mathrm{comm}}$ increasing in $1-\rho$ and
$R_{\max}^{\mathrm{sens}}$ in $\rho$. Since THz communication is
free-space-path-loss limited, the communication link is typically the
binding constraint; the operating point minimizes $\rho$ subject to
$R_{\max}^{\mathrm{sens}}(m,\rho)\ge R_{\mathrm{req}}$, a
communication-centric allocation in which the homodyne gain lets a small
sensing power fraction still meet the ranging requirement.
